Experiments

Start here

The red arrow is the magnetization of a sample of protons, in units of what the magnet makes when the protons have settled: pointing up, along the magnet's field, is equilibrium. The arrow spins round that field, as a top spins round the vertical. It goes far too fast to watch (five million turns a second in the TeachSpin's magnet), so the page slows time down and says by how much.

Drag the arrow's tip to tip it, or press a pulse button: a short burst of radio waves that turns the arrow by an angle you choose. The plots under the sphere are what the instrument records: the voltage the spinning arrow induces in a coil. Switch to the rotating frame to turn with the arrow, so that only the pulses and the slow changes are left to see.

The tabs go on from one spin to many (why the signal dies away, and how an echo brings it back), to the pulse sequences used to measure a sample, and to the Earth's-field board, which does without radio pulses. The buttons at the top set the numbers for the TeachSpin (5 MHz) or the board (2 kHz).

1. The spin precesses about the field at the Larmor frequency

One spin, tipped 45° from the field $B_0$, seen in the lab. It goes round $B_0$ at the Larmor frequency $f_0=\gamma B_0/2\pi$, with $\gamma/2\pi=42.577$ MHz/T for a proton: 5.000 MHz in the TeachSpin's 117.4 mT, slowed here so one turn takes a second. Drag the tip to another angle: the turn takes the same time at any angle. Only the field sets the rate. Under More, change $B_0$: at 234.9 mT it turns twice as fast. The coil lies along $x$, and the voltage in it (the lower plot) follows $dM_x/dt$: an oscillation at $f_0$ as big as the part of the arrow across the field, $|M_{xy}|$.

2. A π/2 pulse tips the spin over, and the coil starts to ring

Equilibrium: the arrow along $B_0$, nothing across it, no signal. A pulse of RF at the Larmor frequency turns it down to the equator. In the lab it spirals down, twelve turns round $B_0$ for each quarter turn down, because the RF field $B_1$ is 50 times weaker than $B_0$. Then it precesses freely, all of it across the field, and the coil rings at full size: the free induction. The pulse lasted 2.50 μs: $\theta=\gamma B_1\tau$ with $B_1=2.35$ mT. Press π/2 about x′ again: a second quarter turn takes it to the bottom, and the ringing stops.

3. The rotating frame: the same pulse without the spinning

Turn with the RF, at the Larmor frequency, and $B_0$ is gone: in this frame nothing moves the spin but $B_1$, which stands still along $x'$. The spiral of experiment 2 becomes a plain quarter turn about $x'$, from $z$ to $y'$. The two plots now say the same thing: the mixer's outputs $M_{x'}$, $M_{y'}$ are the arrow's coordinates in this frame, and the coil's oscillation is that arrow seen from the lab. Try π/2 about y′ from the top: it goes to $-x'$. The axis of the pulse is the phase of the RF.

4. Off resonance: the pulse misses, and the mixer beats

The RF is 50 kHz above the Larmor frequency (half the rate $\gamma B_1/2\pi=100$ kHz at which $B_1$ turns the spin). In the frame of the RF, $B_0$ is no longer quite cancelled: $\Delta B=-2\pi\delta f/\gamma$ is left along $z$, and the spin turns about the tilted effective field $B_\mathrm{eff}$, which misses the equator. The "π/2" leaves $M_z=0.053$ at 50 kHz off, and 0.197 at 100 kHz. After the pulse the spin goes round $z$ at 50 kHz in this frame, so the mixer's outputs swing, 90° apart, while the envelope $|M_{xy}|$ holds still. That beat is how you tune the TeachSpin: turn the frequency until the mixer's output stops beating.

5. Many spins: the signal dies though no spin does

A real sample is many spins, and the magnet's field is not quite the same through it, so their frequencies spread: here over $\Delta f=500$ Hz (full width of a Lorentzian). After a π/2 pulse they all start along $y'$ and fan out, the faster ones (orange) ahead and the slower (violet) behind. The sum, which is what the coil sees, shrinks and dies: the free induction decay, falling by $1/e$ in $T_2^*=1/(\pi\Delta f)=0.64$ ms. No spin has lost anything: each arrow is as long as ever. The spectrum is the line the spread makes, 500 Hz wide at half height: $1/(\pi T_2^*)$.

6. The spin echo: a π pulse brings the signal back

As experiment 5, with a π pulse about $x'$ at $\tau=1.5$ ms, when the fan has spread all the way round. It flips the fan over: the faster spins are now behind by as much as they were ahead, and they catch up at $2\tau$. The fan closes and the signal comes back, an echo (Hahn, 1950), along $-y'$. It is smaller than the FID's start, 0.86 of it, because each spin has also lost some of itself, with $T_2=20$ ms: $e^{-2\tau/T_2}$. The echo undoes the spread but not that. Slide $T_2$ down and the echo shrinks; turn $T_1$ on and watch the sum creep back up towards $z$.

How it works

The Bloch equations. A magnetization $\mathbf M$ in a field $\mathbf B$ precesses and relaxes, $$\frac{d\mathbf M}{dt}=\gamma\,\mathbf M\times\mathbf B-\frac{M_x\hat x+M_y\hat y}{T_2}-\frac{M_z-M_0}{T_1}\,\hat z ,$$ with $\gamma/2\pi=42.577$ MHz/T for protons. The first term turns $\mathbf M$ about $\mathbf B$ at $\omega_0=\gamma B$, clockwise seen from the tip of $\mathbf B$; $T_1$ brings $M_z$ back to equilibrium $M_0$ and $T_2$ takes away the part across the field. The page integrates them exactly, as rotations about the field of the moment and exponentials.

The rotating frame. Seen from a frame turning at $\omega$ about $z$, the same equation holds with $\mathbf B$ replaced by $\mathbf B+\boldsymbol\omega/\gamma$: at $\omega=-\gamma B_0$ (the precession's own sense) $B_0$ vanishes. An RF field $B_1\cos\omega t$ along the coil is two fields rotating in opposite senses; the one that turns with the spins stands still in this frame, along $x'$ (or along the direction its phase says), and turns $\mathbf M$ by $\theta=\gamma B_1\tau$ in a pulse of length $\tau$. The other turns at $2\omega$ and averages away.

The effective field. With the RF at $f_0+\delta f$, what is left of $B_0$ in its frame is $\Delta B=-2\pi\delta f/\gamma$ along $z$, and $\mathbf M$ turns about $\mathbf B_\mathrm{eff}=B_1\hat x'+\Delta B\hat z$, tilted from $z$ by $\beta$ with $\tan\beta=\gamma B_1/2\pi\delta f$, at $\sqrt{(\gamma B_1)^2+(2\pi\delta f)^2}$. From $z$, $M_z=\cos^2\beta+\sin^2\beta\cos\big(\sqrt{1+(2\pi\delta f/\gamma B_1)^2}\,\theta\big)$.

The signal. A coil along $x$ links a flux proportional to $M_x$, and its EMF is $-d\Phi/dt\propto -dM_x/dt$. With $M_x=M_{x'}\cos\omega t+M_{y'}\sin\omega t$, the fast factor dominates, so the EMF is $\omega_0$ times the transverse magnetization: the signal grows as $\omega_0 M_0\propto B_0^2$ (because $M_0\propto B_0$ too). Multiplying it by the reference ($\cos\omega t$ and $\sin\omega t$) and filtering gives $M_{x'}$ and $M_{y'}$: the mixer's outputs. Their length $|M_{xy}|$ is the detector's envelope.

Many spins. The ensemble is 4096 spins whose offsets from $f_0$ follow a Lorentzian of full width $\Delta f$ (at its quantiles, not at random), so that their sum decays as $e^{-t/T_2'}$ with $T_2'=1/(\pi\Delta f)$, and with $T_2$ as $e^{-t/T_2^*}$, $1/T_2^*=1/T_2+1/T_2'$. The sphere shows a few of them, spread evenly through the line. A finite ensemble never quite cancels: a floor of about 1.5% is left after the FID has gone. The power spectrum $|S(f)|^2$ of $A\,e^{-t/T_2^*}$ is a Lorentzian $1/(\pi T_2^*)$ wide at half height.

Spin ½. For one proton the arrow is the expectation value $\langle\mathbf S\rangle$ (times $2/\hbar$) of the state $\cos\frac\theta2\,\text{|↑⟩}+e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac\theta2\,\text{|↓⟩}$: every pure state of a spin ½ is a point on the sphere. Its Hamiltonian $H=-\gamma\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf B$ is linear in $\mathbf S$, and the components of $\mathbf S$ close under commutation, so Ehrenfest's theorem gives $d\langle\mathbf S\rangle/dt=\gamma\langle\mathbf S\rangle\times\mathbf B$ exactly: the quantum spin's arrow moves just as a classical magnetization does, and so does their sum. A sum shorter than one spin is a mixed state.
