1. Which way does it go?

Choose each of the four forms in turn and watch the crests (the white dot rides one). $e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t)}$ and $e^{-i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t)}$ both travel along $\vec k$: they are complex conjugates, the same wave with its colours turning the other way (physicists write the first; electrical engineers write $e^{j(\omega t-\vec k\cdot\vec r)}$, which is the second). When $\vec k\cdot\vec r$ and $\omega t$ have the same sign, the wave travels against $\vec k$. What matters is the relative sign: a crest is where the phase is constant, $\vec k\cdot\vec r=\omega t$, and that moves along $\vec k$ as $t$ grows.

2. At one point, the phase goes round

The little complex plane shows $\psi$ at the centre of the picture: an arrow going round the circle, $\omega/2\pi$ times a second (at speed 1). With $e^{-i\omega t}$ it turns clockwise: that is the sign quantum mechanics fixes, $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$ for energy $E>0$; the two forms with $e^{+i\omega t}$ turn it anticlockwise. Change the phase of $A$ (the slider $\arg A_1$) and every crest shifts sideways: a phase is a position.

3. The real part is what a ripple tank shows

Only $\mathrm{Re}\,\psi=|A|\cos(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t+\varphi)$, in grey: crests light, troughs dark. A real wave needs both $e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t)}$ and its conjugate. A snapshot of the real part cannot tell you which way the wave is going; a snapshot of the complex wave can, once the sign convention is agreed: with $e^{-i\omega t}$ the wave moves towards the side where its phase increases (red, yellow-green, cyan, violet, red again).

4. Standing waves

Two equal waves travelling in opposite directions, $\vec k_2=-\vec k_1$. The sum $2\cos(\vec k\cdot\vec r)\,e^{-i\omega t}$ does not travel at all: its nodes (black lines, half a wavelength apart: $\lambda/2=2.09$ here) stay put, and between them the whole region changes colour together. This is a string, an organ pipe, a particle in a box.

5. Crossed beams make fringes

Two waves of the same wavelength crossing at an angle (60° here). Where they are in step the sum is bright, out of step dark: fringes perpendicular to $\vec k_1-\vec k_2$ (along $\vec k_1+\vec k_2$), spaced by $2\pi/|\vec k_1-\vec k_2|$, which is 4.05 here: at 60° the fringes are as far apart as the crests ($\lambda=4.05$). Drag either arrow to change the angle: the wider it is, the finer the fringes. This is how a hologram is recorded.

6. Beats

Two waves in the same direction with different $|\vec k|$ (1.20 and 1.74 here) and so, with $\omega=c|\vec k|$ as for light, different $\omega$. The sum is a fast wave inside a slowly beating envelope, whose dark bands are $2\pi/\Delta k=11.6$ apart. The envelope moves at $\Delta\omega/\Delta k$ and the crests at $\omega/k$; for light both are $c$, so here the whole pattern slides along together. That is the first step towards a wave packet, where the next mode lets the two speeds differ.

7. Phase velocity and group velocity

A packet of waves near $k_0$, for a free quantum particle, $\omega=\hbar k^2/2m$ (with $\hbar=m=1$). The crests (red dot) move at the phase velocity $\omega/k=k_0/2=0.75$; the packet (blue triangle) at the group velocity $d\omega/dk=k_0=1.5$, twice as fast: the packet overtakes its crests, so they are born at its front and die at its back. (The packet also spreads: each wavelength has its own speed.) On the $\omega(k)$ plot the two velocities are slopes: of the line from the origin to the dot (the red chord), and of the tangent there (the blue line).

8. Deep water waves

$\omega=\sqrt{gk}$: now the group moves at half the phase velocity (0.82 against 1.63), and crests run forward through the packet and vanish at its front, as you can see behind a boat or a duck.

9. Light in vacuum

$\omega=c|k|$: every wavelength moves at the same speed $c$, so the packet keeps its shape and crests ride along with it (both velocities read 1.000). No dispersion.

10. Faster than light, harmlessly

$\omega=\sqrt{c^2k^2+\mu^2}$ with $c=1$: a relativistic particle ($\mu=mc^2/\hbar$), or light in a waveguide or in a plasma ($\mu$ the cut-off or the plasma frequency). The crests move faster than light, at 1.41; the packet, which carries the energy and the information, slower, at 0.71; and $v_\text{phase}\,v_\text{group}=c^2$ (1.41 × 0.71 = 1).

How it works

A plane wave $\psi(\vec r,t)=A\,e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t)}$ has the same value all along each line perpendicular to $\vec k$, and its phase advances by $2\pi$ every wavelength $\lambda=2\pi/|\vec k|$ along $\vec k$. The colours are its phase (red is positive real, yellow-green $+i$, cyan negative real, violet $-i$); the line under the picture gives $|\vec k|$, $\lambda$, $\omega$ (the angular frequency, $2\pi$ times the frequency) and the speed of the crests. The complex amplitude $A=|A|e^{i\varphi}$ sets the height of the wave and where its crests sit. This is the BrainFlux PlaneWave applet, with more to do.

The four forms. The phase is $\pm\vec k\cdot\vec r\pm\omega t$. A crest is where the phase stays constant, so it moves along $\vec k$ when the two signs differ and against $\vec k$ when they agree; $e^{i(\ldots)}$ and $e^{-i(\ldots)}$ are complex conjugates, the same wave with the colours turning the other way. Physicists write $e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t)}$, whose phasor turns clockwise; electrical engineers $e^{j(\omega t-\vec k\cdot\vec r)}$.

Phase velocity, group velocity, dispersion. Crests move at the phase velocity $v_p=\omega/|\vec k|$. A packet, a sum of waves with wavenumbers near $k_0$, moves as a whole at the group velocity $v_g=d\omega/dk$ at $k_0$. On a plot of $\omega(k)$, $v_p$ is the slope of the chord from the origin to $(k_0,\omega_0)$ and $v_g$ the slope of the tangent there. They are equal only when $\omega$ is proportional to $k$ (light in vacuum); otherwise the waves are dispersive: each wavelength has its own speed and the packet spreads.

What is computed. With two waves both have $\omega=c|\vec k|$, as for light, so a standing wave needs equal and opposite $\vec k$. The packet is $\psi(x,t)=\int\tilde\psi(k)\,e^{i(kx-\omega(k)t)}\,dk$ with $\tilde\psi$ a Gaussian about $k_0$ ($|\tilde\psi|^2$ has standard deviation $\Delta k$), summed over 2048 wavenumbers by an FFT; each component runs at $\omega(|k|)$, so the part with $k<0$ goes left. Units: $\hbar=m=1$ for the Schrödinger relation, $c=1$ for light and the relativistic one.
