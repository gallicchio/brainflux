BrainFlux

Interactive demonstrations for physics majors. Each one opens already doing something worth watching; change anything and see what happens. Every demo has a drawer of guided experiments, each of which sets itself up with one click. Demos marked beta are new and still being checked.

2D Fourier transform

Paint on a picture or on its Fourier transform and watch the other change as you paint. Filter the stripes off Fourier's jacket, swap phases between pictures, find the reciprocal lattice of a crystal.

Fourier analysis · optics · crystallography

Schrödinger equation in 1D

Pick a potential and a wave function, or type your own, and watch it evolve: spreading, tunnelling, revivals, Bloch oscillations, measurement and collapse.

quantum mechanics

Scattering in 2D

Send wave packets at disks, wells, slits and crystals. The cross section is measured as they pass; compare it with the Born approximation, see the partial waves, watch the shadow carry out the optical theorem, and turn a crystal until it Bragg-reflects.

quantum mechanics · scattering theory

Plane waves

What $e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec r-\omega t)}$ means: drag the wavevector, try the four sign conventions, superpose waves into standing waves, fringes and beats, and separate phase from group velocity in a packet.

waves · optics · quantum mechanics

Diffraction

Paint an aperture and move the screen: from a sharp shadow to the Fourier transform. The spot of Arago, the Talbot carpet, zone plates, lenses, white light and speckle.

optics · Fourier analysis

Fourier series you can hear

Draw one period of a wave, or shape its harmonics, and listen. Build a square wave term by term, scramble the phases (the ear doesn't mind), and hear the missing fundamental.

Fourier analysis · sound

Maxwell's equations in 2Dbeta

Paint glass, metal and absorbers, place a source, and watch light refract, tunnel through a gap, follow a waveguide, focus and ring in a cavity, solved live from Maxwell's equations. Brewster's angle, waveguide cutoff, photonic band gaps and Cherenkov radiation.

electromagnetism · optics

Radiation from a moving chargebeta

Drag a charge and watch its exact electric field: stop it, and a kink of radiation runs out at the speed of light. Dipoles that lean forward, synchrotron beams, Heaviside's pancake, and Liénard's power against Larmor's.

electromagnetism · relativity

Antenna arraysbeta

Place antennas and set their phases: the far-field pattern is the Fourier transform of the layout. Steer a beam with nothing moving, meet grating lobes, taper the sidelobes away, and focus the waves to a spot.

electromagnetism · Fourier analysis

Radio interferometrybeta

Two antennas listening to the same hiss, one of them a moment later: multiply and average, and the delay survives as a phase. Drag the pair across the uv plane to map the Fourier transform of the sky, see why coherent sources break it, then turn on a whole array and the turning Earth and watch the image come out.

radio astronomy · coherence · Fourier analysis

Interfaces and thin filmsbeta

Stack layers of glass, water, metal or any index you type, and see what they do to light: Brewster's angle, total internal reflection and tunnelling across a gap, soap-film colours as the eye sees them, coatings, Bragg mirrors, Fabry–Pérot filters and surface plasmons.

optics · electromagnetism

Dispersion and Kramers–Kronigbeta

A charge on a spring, shaken by light: in step below a resonance ($n>1$), a quarter period late on it (absorption), against the field above it ($n<1$). See the wave's wavelength and decay inside, why causality ties the index to the absorption one sliver at a time, and pulses whose envelope goes at $c/n_g$: a group delay, a chirp, a peak that leaves early and a front that never does.

optics · electromagnetism · causality

Interferometers and coherencebeta

Move a mirror and count fringes, sweep through the coloured fringes of white light on both sides of equal paths, draw a spectrum and watch its interferogram (or the other way round), resolve the sodium doublet by Fourier-transform spectroscopy, and measure Betelgeuse as Michelson did in 1920.

optics · Fourier analysis · astronomy

Imaging: Abbe and phase contrastbeta

A microscope as two Fourier transforms, drawn from the side as the light goes through it: block or shift the diffraction orders in the back focal plane and watch the image. Abbe's resolution limit, oblique light, dark field, schlieren, and Zernike's phase dot making transparent cells appear.

optics · Fourier analysis · microscopy

Holographybeta

Record how light from an object interferes with a reference beam, then light the recording and watch it send out three beams: the light straight through, the object's own wave coming back from where the object was, and its twin. Zone plates, Gabor's in-line hologram, the three islands of the off-axis transform, parallax through a window, and a hologram cut to pieces.

optics · Fourier analysis

Tomographybeta

How a CT scanner sees inside: watch the sinogram fill as the scanner turns, one angle at a time if you like, back-projection blur and the ramp filter sharpen, and each projection fill a line of Fourier space; point at a spot to see its sinusoid. Streaks from too few angles, the missing wedge, photon noise, aliasing and metal.

imaging · Fourier analysis

Gaussian beams and laser cavitiesbeta

Drag lenses, mirrors and glass along a laser beam and watch its waist, wavefronts and Gouy phase. Then trap it between two mirrors: the stability diagram, rays, the modes a cavity selects, and their frequencies.

optics · lasers

Drums and billiardsbeta

Strike a drum, hear it, and find its modes the way a spectroscopist would, from the Fourier transform of its motion; then draw your own shape and watch it as a quantum billiard: Weyl's law, chaos and level repulsion, scars, and two drums that sound the same.

waves · quantum mechanics · chaos

Quantum phase spacebeta

Watch the Wigner function of a wave function evolve beside a cloud of classical particles under the same Hamiltonian: where they agree, and where interference makes $W$ negative. Coherent, squeezed, number and cat states, tunnelling, the Husimi function and tomography.

quantum mechanics · phase space

The principle of least actionbeta

Bend the true path between two events and watch its action go up, recorded as you go; see when "least" is really a saddle; the twin paradox, Fermat's principle, the brachistochrone, and Feynman's phasors.

Quantum optics: squeezed light and LIGOplanned

One mode of the field in phase space: coherent states and their circle of noise, squeezed states and their ellipse, homodyne detection choosing the quadrature with the local oscillator's phase, and a measurement made with each; then LIGO, reading an arm length as a phase against shot noise, and the squeezed vacuum at its dark port that takes the noise below the shot-noise limit.
