Experiments

Light of wavelength $\lambda$ passes through an aperture you can paint, and lands on a screen a distance $z$ behind it. The light just past the aperture is a sum of plane waves (its Fourier transform), and each travels on with its own phase $e^{ik_zz}$, $k_z=\sqrt{k^2-k_x^2-k_y^2}$. Close to the aperture the screen shows a sharp shadow with fringes at its edges (Fresnel diffraction); far away, the Fourier transform of the aperture itself (Fraunhofer). Which you see depends on the Fresnel number $N_F=a^2/\lambda z$ for an aperture of size $a$: large near, small far.

1. One slit, near and far

Slide $z$ from short to long. At first the slit's shadow is sharp, with ripples inside its edges; then the ripples grow and merge; far away ($N_F\ll1$) the pattern settles into the $\mathrm{sinc}^2$ of a slit, wider the narrower the slit. Press Far field to jump to the limit.

2. The edge of a shadow

A straight edge: light reaches into the geometrical shadow a little, and outside it the brightness oscillates, overshooting by 37% at the first fringe. The fringes are spaced as $\sqrt{\lambda z}$: move the screen back and they spread out.

3. The spot of Arago and Poisson

An opaque disk. At the centre of its shadow is a bright spot. Poisson, judging Fresnel's wave theory in 1818, derived this spot to show the theory absurd; Arago looked, and it was there. Every point of the disk's rim is the same distance from the centre, so the waves from the rim all arrive in step.

4. Young's two slits

Two slits a distance $d$ apart: in the far field, fringes spaced by $\lambda/d$ in angle, inside the single slit's envelope. The side view shows them form: two diverging beams, overlapping and interfering.

5. The Talbot carpet

A grating of period $d$ makes a perfect image of itself at $z_T=2d^2/\lambda$ and a shifted one at half that, with finer and finer patterns between. The side view draws this "Talbot carpet", discovered by Henry Fox Talbot in 1836; the same pattern is the quantum revival of a particle in a box, with $z$ for time.

6. A Fresnel zone plate

Rings, open and closed alternately, with radii $r_n=\sqrt{n\lambda f}$: every open ring sends light to the point $z=f$ on the axis in step, so the plate focuses like a lens (and also at $f/3$, $f/5$...). Watch the side view for the bright focus.

7. A lens is a phase

A thin lens changes only the phase of the light, by $-kr^2/2f$: the aperture is drawn in colour, the colour being the phase. At $z=f$ the light gathers into the focus, the Airy pattern of the lens's round opening.

8. White light

Three wavelengths (red, green, blue) at once. Every wavelength diffracts by a different angle, so fringes come out coloured, and a grating spreads its orders into spectra.

9. Speckle

A random aperture, like rough glass or a laser lit wall: the light arriving at each point is a sum of many waves with random phases, and the screen is a random pattern of bright and dark grains, with the size of the far-field pattern of the whole aperture.

Light and quantum mechanics

For waves near the axis, $k_z\approx k-(k_x^2+k_y^2)/2k$, and the field obeys $\partial_zU=\frac{i}{2k}\nabla_\perp^2U$: the free Schrödinger equation, with $z$ for time and $1/k$ for $\hbar/m$. Diffraction is the spreading of a wave packet. (This demo uses the exact $k_z$, not the approximation.)
