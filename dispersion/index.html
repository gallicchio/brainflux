Experiments

A light wave $e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$ shakes the charges in a medium, and their response adds to the field: $\varepsilon(\omega)=1+\chi(\omega)$. Here the charges are damped oscillators, $$\varepsilon(\omega)=1+\sum_j\frac{\omega_{pj}^2}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2-i\gamma_j\omega},$$ each with its resonance $\omega_j$, its width $\gamma_j$ and its strength $\omega_{pj}^2=f_j\omega_p^2$ ($\omega_j=0$ is a free electron: a Drude metal). The wave then travels as $e^{i(\tilde n\omega z/c-\omega t)}$ with $\tilde n=n+i\kappa=\sqrt{\varepsilon}$: $n$ sets its speed, $\kappa$ its absorption, and $R=|(\tilde n-1)/(\tilde n+1)|^2$ is the fraction reflected at normal incidence. Drag the round handles to move a resonance or change its strength, the small ones to change its width; point at a plot (or tap it) to read the numbers at one frequency. The three views: the medium, Kramers–Kronig (draw $\mathrm{Im}\,\chi$ or $\mathrm{Re}\,\chi$ and get the other, and the response in time), and pulses sent through it, computed exactly frequency by frequency. With $c=1$, a frequency unit $\omega_u$ makes $1/\omega_u$ the unit of time and $c/\omega_u$ that of distance; the real materials are in eV, MHz or THz, with times and distances to match.

1. One resonance

One line at $\omega_0=2$. $\varepsilon''$ (orange) is the absorption line, a peak of width $\gamma$. $\varepsilon'$ (blue) rises towards the line from both sides: that is normal dispersion, $dn/d\omega>0$, where light is not absorbed. Inside the line it falls: anomalous dispersion, and only where the medium absorbs. Just above the line $\varepsilon'$ dips below zero and $n$ below 1, and there the reflectance peaks (21% at $\omega=2.1$). Drag the line about, make it wider or stronger, add a second one, switch to $n$ and $\kappa$.

2. A pulse is late: the group velocity

A pulse with its spectrum (the grey hump) well below the line, sent through $z=120$ of the medium, against the same pulse through vacuum (dashed). It arrives late by $z(n_g-1)/c$, where $n_g=n+\omega\,dn/d\omega=c\,dk/d\omega$ is the group index (green): $n_g=1.25$, so 30.0 late, and the peak comes at 29.8. The crests inside travel at $c/n$ ($n=1.15$), faster than the pulse. The tail of the line still absorbs a little ($\kappa=0.005$): by $z=120$ the peak is down to 54%. Drag the carrier (the vertical line) towards the line: $n_g$ grows, the pulse is later, and it spreads, because its two sides see different $n_g$; its front turns red (lower frequencies go faster here).

3. A short pulse spreads, red first

A 10 fs pulse at 800 nm through fused silica, in the frame moving with the pulse. In the visible and near infrared glass has normal dispersion, so red light (lower $\omega$, lower $n_g$) runs ahead of blue: the pulse spreads and is chirped, red at its front and blue at its back (the colour of the envelope is the local frequency). Its length (r.m.s., in the read-out) grows by $\sqrt2$ in 1 mm, doubles in 1.73 mm and is 5.1 times its start after 5 mm, just as $\sqrt{1+(z/L)^2}$ with $L=T_0^2/\beta_2=1.0$ mm says, for $\beta_2=d^2k/d\omega^2=36\ \mathrm{fs^2/mm}$. This is why femtosecond lasers need prism or grating compressors. Meanwhile the whole pulse is 7.8 ps behind light in vacuum.

4. Glass: why a prism spreads blue more

Fused silica, as three oscillators (Malitson's fit): two resonances in the ultraviolet (10.7 and 18 eV), one in the infrared (0.13 eV, at the left edge). The visible band (the rainbow strip) lies between them, where nothing absorbs and $n$ rises gently towards the ultraviolet lines: $n=1.456$ in the red (656 nm), $1.463$ in the blue (486 nm). So blue is bent more. The rise steepens towards the resonance, which is why violet is spread more than red. Tap the plot to read $n$ at any frequency.

5. A metal and its plasma frequency

Free electrons, $\varepsilon=1-\omega_p^2/(\omega^2+i\gamma\omega)$, for sodium: $\hbar\omega_p=5.9$ eV. Below $\omega_p$, $\varepsilon'<0$, $\tilde n$ is almost purely imaginary, the wave dies within a skin depth and $R$ is nearly 1: a mirror. Above $\omega_p$ the electrons cannot keep up, $\varepsilon'>0$ and the metal turns transparent. Sodium, like the other alkali metals, is transparent in the ultraviolet below about 210 nm (Wood, 1933). Drag the $\omega_p$ handle; widen $\gamma$ to see the edge soften.

6. The ionosphere reflects AM radio

The same physics with $10^{12}$ electrons per cubic metre: $f_p=\omega_p/2\pi\approx9$ MHz (frequencies here are $f$ in MHz). AM broadcasts (0.5 to 1.7 MHz) are below $f_p$ and bounce back, which is why they are heard far beyond the horizon at night. FM (around 100 MHz) and satellite signals go straight through. This is the idealised sharp surface at normal incidence; the real ionosphere is gradual, and reflects each frequency where $f_p$ of the local density reaches it. At a slant it reflects frequencies up to $f_p/\cos\theta$, which is how shortwave goes round the world.

7. Reststrahlen: a mirror between $\omega_\mathrm{TO}$ and $\omega_\mathrm{LO}$

Rock salt in the infrared (frequencies in THz). The ions vibrate against each other at the transverse optical frequency $\omega_\mathrm{TO}=4.9$ THz; the electrons respond instantly at these frequencies and add the background $\varepsilon_\infty=2.34$. Between $\omega_\mathrm{TO}$ and the zero of $\varepsilon'$ at $\omega_\mathrm{LO}=\omega_\mathrm{TO}\sqrt{\varepsilon(0)/\varepsilon_\infty}=7.8$ THz (Lyddane, Sachs and Teller) $\varepsilon'<0$ and the crystal reflects almost everything. Repeated reflections off such crystals filter out all but these "residual rays" (Rubens, 1897).

8. Draw the absorption, get the index

Here $\mathrm{Im}\,\chi$ is a flat band of absorption, drawn by hand, and $\mathrm{Re}\,\chi$ is what causality makes of it: $$\mathrm{Re}\,\chi(\omega)=\frac1\pi\,P\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\chi(\omega')}{\omega'-\omega}\,d\omega'$$ (Kramers–Kronig), with logarithmic peaks at the sharp edges of the band. Draw on $\mathrm{Im}\,\chi$: an absorption line anywhere gives normal dispersion everywhere else. Or draw on $\mathrm{Re}\,\chi$ and see what absorption it needs (orange below zero would be gain). The bottom plot is $\chi(t)$, the response to a kick at $t=0$: zero before it. The transform is done with FFTs on a grid out to 8 times the plotted range; an error of about 0.1% comes from that grid.

9. Break causality

A medium that absorbs exactly as the single line does, but with $\mathrm{Re}\,\chi=0$: no dispersion at all. Its response $\chi(t)$ is odd in time: it starts responding before the kick, as much as after. Kramers–Kronig is causality written in frequency: a response that vanishes for $t<0$ has real and imaginary parts that are Hilbert transforms of each other. With Break causality on, draw on either side and watch $\chi(t)$ leak into the past; turn it off and the other side is recomputed, and the past goes quiet.

10. The f-sum rule

However a line is drawn, its area is fixed: $$\int_0^\infty\omega\,\varepsilon''(\omega)\,d\omega=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\omega_p^2,\qquad \omega_p^2=\frac{Ne^2}{\varepsilon_0m}.$$ Drag a line's width handles: it gets lower as it gets wider and the read-out does not move. Move it: still the same. Only the strength (the number of electrons) changes it. This is Kramers–Kronig at high frequency, where every electron responds as if it were free, $\chi\to-\omega_p^2/\omega^2$.

11. Faster than light? The peak exits early

A long pulse tuned to the middle of a weak absorption line, where $dn/d\omega<0$ so steeply that $n_g$ is negative ($-0.98$). The peak of what comes out leaves the medium before the peak of a pulse through vacuum arrives: 6.7 time units early at $z=5$, 12.9 at $z=10$ (the group velocity alone would say 9.9 and 19.7; the absorption reshapes the pulse too). But it is mostly absorbed: by $z=10$ the peak is 18% as high (the curve is scaled up to be seen). Nothing is going faster than light: the medium absorbs the back of the pulse more than its front, and the front was already there, in the smooth Gaussian, long before the peak.

12. ...and a front never arrives early

The same pulse, cut off sharply in front: before the cut, 24 before its peak, there is no field at all. Now what comes out piles up just behind the front, and the front itself arrives exactly when light through vacuum would: before it the field is below $10^{-9}$ of the peak (the read-out; switch on log |E| to see it). Information travels with fronts, and fronts travel at $c$: as $\omega\to\infty$ every medium has $n\to1$. (The cut is smoothed over a couple of time steps of the computation, a small fraction of a period.)

13. Precursors

A carrier switched on suddenly at $t=0$ into a dense medium (Brillouin's own numbers, 1914). The first thing to arrive, exactly at $t=z/c$, is the Sommerfeld precursor: the highest frequencies, which see $n\approx1$, wiggling slower and slower. Next comes the Brillouin precursor, the lowest frequencies, a slow swing near $t=n(0)z/c$ ($n(0)=1.5$), and only then does the field settle into the signal at the carrier frequency, weakened by absorption. Move the carrier above the line, into the band where it is absorbed (at $\omega=5$, $e^{-\kappa\omega z/c}=e^{-53}$ by $z=10$), and only the precursors get through. Frequencies are in units of $10^{16}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, so $z=10$ is 0.3 µm.

14. Type χ(ω): the sign of i

$\chi(\omega)=\omega_p^2/(\omega_0^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega)$ typed in (as wp^2/(w0^2 - w^2 - i g w)). Its pole is at $\omega=-i\gamma/2\pm\ldots$, in the lower half-plane, and $\chi(t)$ is causal. Change $-i$ to $+i$: the pole moves to the upper half-plane, $\chi(t)$ lives entirely in the past, and $\mathrm{Im}\,\chi<0$, which is gain. (With $e^{+i\omega t}$, the engineers' convention, the signs are the other way round.) Try the others: a constant is an instantaneous response, $\delta(t)$; absorption with no dispersion is not causal. A conductor, $i\sigma/\omega$ (i s/w), comes out not quite causal either: $\chi(t)=\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\mathrm{sgn}\,t$. The causal one is $i\sigma/(\omega+i0)$, whose real part has a $\pi\sigma\,\delta(\omega)$ that no formula sampled on a grid can show; with it, $\chi(t)=\sigma$ for $t>0$ and 0 before.

Approximations: the medium is linear and its response local (no spatial dispersion); the pulse is a plane wave already inside the medium (no reflection at its surface); the time grid is periodic and wide enough that nothing wraps round.
